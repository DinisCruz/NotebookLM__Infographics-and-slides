# The 100% Cost for 10% Use Problem (Software Bloat and Inefficiency)

> *Semantic Knowledge Graph (SKG) - markdown serialization for search, discovery, and graph database integration*

---

## Summary

This white paper analyzes the fundamental inefficiency where organizations pay 100% of software costs but use only ~10% of features, proposing focused "10% solutions" as an alternative. The document explores how open-source foundations, LLM-enabled development, and Europe's distributed market create opportunities for tailored software at fraction of traditional costs. Key insight: unused features aren't just waste — they become "feature gravity" that creates vendor lock-in, with enterprises fearing they might someday need that advanced feature; the solution is unbundling big software through specialized open-source alternatives that deliver 100% of needed functionality at 10% of cost.

---

## Key Concepts

- **Feature Bloat**: Software packages loaded with capabilities users don't use — in Microsoft Word, majority of features sit idle while only core 10% are used daily.

- **Feature Gravity/Lock-in**: Unused features become switching barriers — enterprises fear they might need that advanced feature, so they stick with heavy expensive solutions "just in case."

- **10% Solutions**: Highly focused software delivering exactly the features users actually need at roughly 10% of the cost, flipping the equation to "10% cost for 100% of your use case."

- **Unbundling**: Picking off narrow slices of big software suites where users actually get value, doing that slice better with none of the excess.

- **LLM-Enabled Development**: AI-assisted solo developers or tiny teams achieving what previously required large teams, making custom solutions economically viable.

- **Digital Sovereignty**: Organizations running software on their own terms without being at mercy of distant vendor's pricing or policies — enabled by open-source self-hosted alternatives.

---

## Core Arguments

1. Organizations pay for far more functionality than they use — studies show ~10% feature utilization while paying 100% of costs, with over 50% of IT pros reporting unused SaaS features.

2. Feature bloat creates vendor lock-in through "feature gravity" — customers hesitate to switch to simpler alternatives because they fear losing features they might someday need.

3. Open-source foundations plus LLM-enabled development now make focused solutions economically viable — a one-person AI-augmented team can deliver functional products for small regional markets.

4. Europe's distributed market (diverse languages, regulations, local needs) is actually a strategic advantage — creating thousands of micro-markets where focused 10% solutions can thrive.

5. The model creates a "service-oriented revenue" business where local companies earn through customization, deployment, hosting and support rather than licenses — keeping money in regional economies.

6. Quality engineering discipline is MORE important with AI assistance — the goal is professional-grade engineering, not quick demos; maintainability, security, and user-centric design remain paramount.
