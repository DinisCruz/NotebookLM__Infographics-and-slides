The Physics of Meaning: Why We Stopped Building Software and Started Fighting Entropy

We are spending billions trying to "un-bake the cake" of enterprise data. It’s time to fix the ingredients.


This past week, I’ve been engaged in some of the most rigorous public debates of my career. From data architects to security researchers, the consensus is shifting. We are realizing that the “Data Management” era is over, and the “Data Physics” era has begun.

We used to think of data issues as “bugs” to be fixed. We now realize they are Entropy—a fundamental force of chaos that degrades meaning the moment it leaves the source.

Reflecting on these conversations, three distinct themes emerged that explain why we built the Semantic Data Charter (SDC) the way we did.

1. The Fallacy of Unstructured Data (The Cake)
The industry is currently obsessed with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). We are building massive pipelines to ingest PDFs, emails, and reports, hoping AI can reconstruct the meaning hidden inside.

But as I wrote on LinkedIn, this is an attempt to “un-bake the cake.”

Data is rarely born unstructured. It starts as a form entry, a sensor reading, or a transaction. It is born structured. It only becomes “unstructured” because we compress it into documents for human consumption. We strip away metadata and context to make it readable to people, effectively destroying its value to machines.

We are breaking the data, and then paying LLM companies millions to glue it back together.




The Fix: We need to capture the data in its Native Semantic State. If we preserve the structure at the moment of capture, we don’t need to hallucinate the context later. We stop fighting entropy; we prevent it.

2. The Refraction of Truth (The Prism)
When we try to explain SDC, people often ask: “Is it for AI? Is it for Compliance? Is it for APIs?”

The answer is Yes, because SDC acts like a Prism.




We treat the SDC4 Protocol as the “White Light”—the raw, unadulterated intent of the domain expert. When that single source of truth hits our engine, it refracts into three distinct beams of value:

The Cyan Beam (Development): Zero-Cost Evolution. We use additive modeling so you never have to refactor an API or migrate a schema again.

The Gold Beam (Legal): The Liability Shield. We seal data within its original semantic context, creating a “Digital Alibi” that protects professionals from retroactive judgment.

The Magenta Beam (AI): Hallucination Defense. We feed the model self-describing data, replacing probabilistic guessing with deterministic knowledge.

One input. Three architectural outputs.

3. Security via Simplicity (The Fortress)
Perhaps the most critical realization this week came from looking at the security research of experts like Dinis Cruz.

In a traditional enterprise architecture, “Complexity is the Enemy.” An application might have 500+ REST API endpoints. That is 500 doors that a security team has to lock, monitor, and patch. That is 500 chances for a Broken Object Level Authorization (BOLA) attack.

We decided to cheat.

Because SDC separates the Structure (XML/XSD) from the Meaning (RDF), we don’t need unique endpoints for “Patients” vs. “Invoices” vs. “Suspects.”

We collapsed the entire attack surface down to two well-guarded gates:

GET /schema (Here is the map).

POST /ingest (Here is the payload).

That’s it.

By enforcing strict Validation at the ingest gate, we reject malformed data before it ever touches the application logic. We don’t have an attack surface; we have an attack point.

The Conclusion
Whether we are talking about Data Entropy (Quality), Refraction (Value), or Surface Area (Security), the lesson is the same:

You cannot solve these problems downstream.

You cannot “Secure” a sprawling API after it’s built. You cannot “Govern” data after it has been flattened into a PDF. You cannot “Fix” context after the domain expert has retired.

You have to solve it at the Source. You have to capture the intent when it happens.
