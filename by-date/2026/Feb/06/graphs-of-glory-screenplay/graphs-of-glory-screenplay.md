# GRAPHS OF GLORY

## A Comedy in Three Acts

**Genre:** Tech Comedy / Buddy Film / Existential Crisis  
**Tagline:** "When your text is just a string, everything falls apart."  
**Runtime:** 117 minutes  
**Rating:** PG-13 (contains intense whiteboard scenes and gratuitous use of the word "ontology")

# ACT ONE: "THE STRING PROBLEM"

## SCENE 1: THE INCITING INCIDENT

*INT. STARTUP OFFICE - NIGHT*

*The office is empty except for DINO, late 30s, disheveled, surrounded by empty coffee cups and Post-it notes. He's staring at a screen showing an issue tracker.*

**DINO**  
*(muttering)*  
Bug. The type says Bug. But the title says "Add dark mode support."  
*(louder)*  
THAT'S NOT A BUG. THAT'S A FEATURE REQUEST.

*He stands up, paces.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Why can't the system SEE this? It's right there! In the WORDS!

*He gestures at the screen like it personally betrayed him.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
The type field connects to a node. The title field connects to... a STRING. A STRING!  
*(whispering, horrified)*  
The graph... stops at the text.

*DRAMATIC MUSIC STING*

*He reaches for his phone, hits record.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Voice memo. February something. I've discovered something terrible. Text is... opaque. The graph can't see inside it. If meaning comes from connectivity... then text has no meaning.  
*(pause)*  
Oh god. I've been living a lie.

*He slumps into his chair.*

---

## SCENE 2: THE WHITEBOARD REVELATION

*INT. CONFERENCE ROOM - DAY*

*DINO stands before a massive whiteboard covered in boxes and arrows. His colleague MARCUS, a skeptical senior engineer, sits at the table eating a sandwich.*

**MARCUS**  
So let me get this straight. You want to turn every piece of text into a graph.

**DINO**  
Not want. NEED. Look.

*He draws furiously on the whiteboard.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Issue-42. Type: Bug. Title: "Login fails on mobile."

**MARCUS**  
I see it.

**DINO**  
No, you DON'T see it. That's the POINT. The graph doesn't see "login." It doesn't see "fails." It doesn't see "mobile." It just sees...  
*(dramatic pause)*  
...a string.

**MARCUS**  
*(chewing)*  
So?

**DINO**  
SO? SO?!  
*(he grabs Marcus's sandwich, holds it up)*  
What if I told you this sandwich... was just a string to me? What if I couldn't see the bread, the lettuce, the mysterious brown meat?

**MARCUS**  
Give me back my sandwich.

**DINO**  
*(ignoring him)*  
The sandwich is a GRAPH, Marcus. Bread contains lettuce. Lettuce adjacent to meat. Meat of uncertain provenance. Each component has MEANING through its CONNECTIONS.

**MARCUS**  
*(standing, taking sandwich back)*  
I'm going to HR.

**DINO**  
*(grabbing Marcus's arm)*  
What if text... was also a graph?

*Beat.*

**MARCUS**  
I'm definitely going to HR.

## SCENE 3: THE O&T DISASTER

*INT. DINO'S HOME OFFICE - NIGHT*

*DINO is running experiments. His screen shows the results of an LLM extraction - nodes and edges everywhere, complete chaos.*

**DINO**  
Extract the semantic graph from this text.

*He hits enter. Watches the results populate.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Login. Button. Mobile. Device. Fails. Response. Users. Report. iOS. Safari. Approximately. Fifteen. Percent...

*The screen fills with hundreds of nodes.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
No. No no no. That's not... you can't just...

*He stands up, knocking over a coffee cup.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
IT EXTRACTED THE WORD "THE"! THERE'S A NODE FOR "THE"!

*His SPOUSE, ALEX, appears in the doorway in pajamas.*

**ALEX**  
It's 2 AM. Why are you yelling about definite articles?

**DINO**  
I asked the LLM to extract meaning and it gave me... EVERYTHING. There's no structure. No constraints. It just...  
*(gesturing wildly)*  
...sprawled.

**ALEX**  
Sprawled.

**DINO**  
SPRAWLED, ALEX. Semantic sprawl. It's like asking someone to clean the house and they just... move everything to a different room.

**ALEX**  
Come to bed.

**DINO**  
*(grabbing phone, recording)*  
Voice memo. 2 AM. I've made a terrible mistake. You can't just extract everything. You need... constraints. A contract. You have to define what you're looking for BEFORE you look for it.  
*(revelation dawning)*  
Ontology first. Taxonomy first. O&T first!

**ALEX**  
*(leaving)*  
I'm going to sleep. Don't O&T too loud.

**DINO**  
*(calling after)*  
O&T FIRST, ALEX! THIS CHANGES EVERYTHING!

*YOUNG DINO sits at a desk. Tests are running. They're all failing.*

**YOUNG DINO**

**YOUNG DINO**

Not to the test. The test is looking for "Submit."

*YOUNG DINO stares at the screen in horror.*

**YOUNG DINO (V.O.)**

*SMASH CUT TO:*

*PRESENT DAY - DINO bolts upright in bed.*

**DINO**  
OH NO.

**ALEX**  
*(half asleep)*  
What.

**DINO**  
I almost made the same mistake. I was going to write tests that check if the risk description "mentions" a remediation. But what if they say "mitigation" instead? Or "countermeasure"? Or "fix"?

**ALEX**  
Those are all the same thing.

**DINO**  
EXACTLY! But a text test wouldn't know that! I almost Gherkin'd myself!

**ALEX**  
That sounds inappropriate.

**DINO**  
*(getting out of bed)*  
I need to call Claude.

**ALEX**  
It's 4 AM.

**DINO**  
Claude doesn't sleep, Alex. Claude is ALWAYS THERE.

# ACT TWO: "THE FIVE LAYERS"

## SCENE 5: THE ARCHITECTURE DRAWINGS

*INT. CO-WORKING SPACE - DAY*

*DINO has rented an entire conference room. The walls are covered with diagrams. ASCII art everywhere. Boxes. Arrows. So many arrows.*

*His friend and fellow architect, PETRA, stands in the doorway, stunned.*

**PETRA**  
What... happened here?

**DINO**  
I've been drawing.

**PETRA**  
I can see that. There are diagrams on the CEILING.

**DINO**  
*(excited)*  
Look. System context diagram. Container diagram. Component diagram. Data flow. State machine.

*He gestures at each wall in turn.*

**PETRA**  
Is this for a real system?

**DINO**  
Food delivery platform. Customers, restaurants, drivers. API Gateway. Microservices. The whole thing.

**PETRA**  
Why?

**DINO**  
*(grabbing her shoulders)*  
Because DIAGRAMS are also GRAPHS, Petra. Boxes are nodes. Arrows are edges. And if diagrams are graphs, and text is graphs...

**PETRA**  
Please let go of me.

**DINO**  
*(releasing her)*  
...then we can COMPARE them. We can ask: does what I drew match what I wrote? Does what I wrote match what got built?

**PETRA**  
That's... actually interesting.

**DINO**  
It's MORE than interesting, Petra. It's the key to everything. We've been describing systems in different languages - visual, natural, code - and just ASSUMING they agree.

**PETRA**  
They usually don't.

**DINO**  
THEY NEVER DO! And nobody notices until something breaks!  
*(beat)*  
I call it... Compatibility Through Connectivity.

**PETRA**  
That's a terrible name.

**DINO**  
I know. I love it.

## SCENE 6: THE C4 CONFRONTATION

*INT. TECH CONFERENCE - DAY*

*DINO is in the audience at a talk about the C4 Model. The speaker, DR. QUADRANT, a pompous architecture guru, is on stage.*

**DR. QUADRANT**  
And so, we have four levels. Context. Container. Component. Code. Four levels, perfectly nested, perfectly organized.

*DINO's eye twitches.*

**DR. QUADRANT (CONT'D)**  
Everything fits into these four boxes. That's the beauty of it.

*DINO stands up.*

**DINO**  
Question.

**DR. QUADRANT**  
*(smiling)*  
Yes?

**DINO**  
What if my component is itself a system? What if I need to zoom into it and it has its own containers? And components? And those have sub-components?

**DR. QUADRANT**  
Well, you just... you document it at the appropriate level.

**DINO**  
But which level IS appropriate? What if I'm standing at the enterprise level and your "container" is invisible because it's too deep? But from inside that container, it's a whole SYSTEM with its own architecture?

*Murmuring in the audience.*

**DR. QUADRANT**  
The four levels are a guide, not a—

**DINO**  
*(building steam)*  
Four is arbitrary! It's like saying there are exactly four levels of zoom on a map! What about street view? What about seeing the individual BRICKS?

**DR. QUADRANT**  
Sir, I'm going to have to ask you to—

**DINO**  
The levels should EMERGE from TRAVERSAL! A node doesn't KNOW what level it's at! It just knows what it contains and what contains it! The level is a VIEW, not a PROPERTY!

*Security approaches.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(as he's escorted out)*  
YOU'VE MADE THE SAME MISTAKE AS SCHEMA.ORG! YOU'RE TRYING TO FIT REALITY INTO BOXES! BUT REALITY IS FRACTAL!

*The doors close behind him.*

*Beat.*

**DR. QUADRANT**  
*(to audience)*  
So... as I was saying... four levels...

## SCENE 7: THE COMPATIBILITY EPIPHANY

*EXT. PARK BENCH - DAY*

*DINO sits on a bench, dejected. PETRA sits next to him, holding two coffees.*

**PETRA**  
So you got kicked out of another conference.

**DINO**  
They don't UNDERSTAND, Petra. Everyone's describing systems in their own language. The architect writes docs. The architect draws diagrams. The developer writes code. DevOps writes configs. The system generates traces. Five languages! For the same thing!

**PETRA**  
And?

**DINO**  
And nobody's checking if they AGREE!

*He takes the coffee.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
What if there was a way to extract a graph from each one? Text graph. Diagram graph. Code graph. Config graph. Trace graph. And then compare them?

**PETRA**  
Like a compatibility test?

**DINO**  
*(freezing mid-sip)*  
What did you say?

**PETRA**  
A compatibility test? Like, do they all describe the same thing?

**DINO**  
*(slowly putting down coffee)*  
Petra. That's it. That's the question. Not "does the code work?" but "do all the representations AGREE?"

**PETRA**  
That's what I—

**DINO**  
*(grabbing phone)*  
Voice memo. Compatibility testing. Not checking if code is correct. Checking if text, diagram, code, config, and traces all tell the same story!  
*(to Petra)*  
We're not testing correctness. We're testing ALIGNMENT.

**PETRA**  
You're welcome.

**DINO**  
*(still recording)*  
The question is: does the system work the way the architect THINKS it works?

*He stands up, energized.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
I need to write this down. All of it. The five layers. The comparison. The meaning through agreement...

*He rushes off, leaving Petra on the bench.*

**PETRA**  
*(calling after him)*  
You left your coffee!  
*(beat)*  
You left your PHONE!

*She looks at the phone, still recording.*

**PETRA (CONT'D)**  
*(into phone)*  
This is Petra. Dino has lost his mind but I think he might be onto something. End of voice memo.

# ACT THREE: "THE LLM GAMBIT"

## SCENE 8: THE IMPLEMENTATION DESPAIR

*INT. DINO'S OFFICE - NIGHT*

*DINO is surrounded by architecture documents. Beautiful, comprehensive documents. But he looks defeated.*

*MARCUS enters.*

**MARCUS**  
You look terrible.

**DINO**  
I've designed the perfect system, Marcus. TextExtractor. DiagramExtractor. CodeExtractor. CompatibilityEngine. ReportGenerator. It's beautiful.

**MARCUS**  
So what's the problem?

**DINO**  
I have to BUILD it. Do you know how much code that is? NLP pipelines. AST parsers. Graph comparison algorithms. It'll take months. Maybe years.

**MARCUS**  
So?

**DINO**  
So I can't USE it until I BUILD it. And I can't know if the DESIGN is right until I USE it. It's a bootstrap problem!

*He slumps in his chair.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
I've designed a beautiful machine that I can't afford to build.

**MARCUS**  
*(sitting down)*  
Remember when we had to prototype the payment flow?

**DINO**  
Yeah, we used a Wizard of Oz approach. Someone manually did what the system would do.

**MARCUS**  
Right. The user didn't know there was a person behind the curtain.

*Beat.*

**DINO**  
*(slowly looking up)*  
Marcus. You beautiful genius.

**MARCUS**  
I am?

**DINO**  
What if I don't need to build the TextExtractor? What if I just... DESCRIBE what it should do... and have an LLM ACT LIKE it?

**MARCUS**  
That's... actually clever.

**DINO**  
*(standing, excited)*  
I write: "You are the TextExtractor. Given this text, extract the semantic graph." And it DOES it! The caller doesn't know if it's real code or an LLM!

**MARCUS**  
But it won't be as fast or as precise—

**DINO**  
IT DOESN'T MATTER! I can validate the DESIGN! I can run the WORKFLOW! I can discover what's BROKEN before I write a single line of code!

*He grabs Marcus's shoulders.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
The LLM is the execution engine for code that doesn't exist yet!

**MARCUS**  
Please stop grabbing me.

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(reading response)*  
"I understand. I am now the TextExtractor. Please provide the text to analyze."  
*(to himself)*  
IT WORKS.

*He opens another window.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(typing)*  
You are the DiagramExtractor component. When given an ASCII architecture diagram, you will extract the structural graph...

*ALEX appears in the doorway.*

**ALEX**  
Are you talking to yourself?

**DINO**  
I'm talking to AGENTS. Role agents. Each one is pretending to be a component of my system.

**ALEX**  
So... you've invented imaginary friends. For software.

**DINO**  
They're not imaginary! They're SIMULATIONS! Look!

*He pulls ALEX over.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
This one extracts text. This one extracts diagrams. This one compares them for compatibility. And THIS one...  
*(dramatic pause)*  
...generates the report.

**ALEX**  
And they all work?

**DINO**  
They all FUNCTION. The TextExtractor gives me a graph. The DiagramExtractor gives me a graph. The CompatibilityEngine tells me if they agree!

**ALEX**  
Without writing any code?

**DINO**  
WITHOUT WRITING ANY CODE!

*He stands up triumphantly.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
The prompt IS the specification. The LLM IS the implementation. And when I understand the pattern well enough, THEN I write the code!

**ALEX**  
*(impressed)*  
That's... actually smart.

**DINO**  
I KNOW!  
*(beat)*  
I need to write this down. The LLM as execution engine. The gradual replacement. The—

**ALEX**  
It's 1 AM.

**DINO**

Sleep is for people who haven't solved the bootstrap problem, Alex.

**DINO**  
Ladies and gentlemen. I present to you: Compatibility Testing.

**ENGINEER 1**

**DINO**  
Yes. And I was RIGHT.  
*(clicking to next slide)*  
Here's our architecture. Container diagram. Text descriptions. Architecture rules.

**ENGINEER 2**  
We've seen this.

**DINO**  
You've SEEN it. But have you TESTED it?

*Confused murmurs.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
The text says "All external traffic must pass through the API Gateway." The diagram shows... what?

*He zooms in on the diagram.*

**ENGINEER 1**  
The apps connect to the gateway, which connects to the services.

**DINO**  
Correct! Now. Are they COMPATIBLE?

**ENGINEER 2**  
They... seem like it?

**DINO**  
SEEM! That's the problem! We've been ASSUMING compatibility! Watch this.

*He types into his system.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
TextExtractor: extract the semantic graph.

*A graph appears on screen.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
DiagramExtractor: extract the diagram graph.

*Another graph appears.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
CompatibilityEngine: assess compatibility for rule "all external traffic through gateway."

*A report generates.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(reading)*  
"Text: FOUND. Explicit rule statement. Diagram: FOUND. All client apps connect through gateway, no bypass paths. Compatibility: FULL."

Yes.

**DINO (CONT'D)**

It catches EVERYTHING. Because we're not testing if code works. We're testing if our understanding is ALIGNED.

**MARCUS**

You absolutely did not.

## SCENE 12: THE FINAL VOICE MEMO

*INT. DINO'S HOME OFFICE - NIGHT*

*DINO sits alone, looking at his phone. The documents are done. The system works. He hits record.*

**DINO**  
Final voice memo. I think.

*Beat.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
This whole thing started because text was opaque. The graph stopped at the string boundary. Meaning ended where words began.

*He pauses.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
And I thought: that's wrong. Meaning doesn't stop. It flows. From word to concept. From concept to definition. From definition to other definitions. All connected. All the way down.

*He stands, walks to the window.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Then I realized: it's not just text. It's everything. Diagrams. Code. Configs. Traces. They're all trying to describe the same thing. In different languages. And we just... HOPE they agree.

*He turns back.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
We hope, but we don't check. We assume, but we don't verify. And then something breaks, and we're surprised.

*He sits back down.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
Not anymore. Now we can ask the real question: Does the system work the way we THINK it works?

*He stops the recording. Looks at it. Then smiles.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(to himself)*  
Meaning through connectivity. All the way across.

*He sets down the phone. The screen shows the document library: all the architecture documents, neatly organized.*

**DINO (CONT'D)**  
*(quietly)*  
Now we know.

*FADE TO BLACK.*
